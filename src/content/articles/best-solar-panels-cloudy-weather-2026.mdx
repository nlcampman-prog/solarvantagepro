"Solar doesn't work where I live — it's too cloudy."

It's the most common objection to solar power, and it's wrong. Solar panels do work on cloudy days. They work in the Pacific Northwest, in the UK, in the grayest corners of northern Europe and the US Northeast. They just work differently — and the panel technology you choose matters far more in overcast conditions than it does under full sun.

In direct sunlight, most quality panels perform similarly. Under clouds, the differences between panel types, cell technologies, and charge controllers become the difference between a usable charge and a trickle that barely moves the needle.

Here's what you need to know about solar panels in cloudy conditions in 2026 — which technologies actually deliver, and which ones to pick when the sun is more of a suggestion than a guarantee.

## Solar Works on Cloudy Days — Here's How Much

First, let's set expectations. On a heavily overcast day, solar panels produce **10–25% of their rated output**. A 100W panel that delivers 100W under full, direct sun might deliver 10–25W under thick cloud cover.

But "10–25%" isn't the whole story. Different panel technologies perform at different ends of that range. A standard polycrystalline panel might hit 10% of rated output under clouds. A high-efficiency monocrystalline panel with an MPPT controller might hit 20–25%. Over the course of a Pacific Northwest winter, that difference means the panel either keeps your battery topped off — or doesn't.

| Condition | Typical Output (% of rated) | What It Means for a 200W Setup |
|---|---|---|
| Full direct sun | 100% | 200W |
| Light cloud cover | 40–60% | 80–120W |
| Heavy overcast | 10–25% | 20–50W |
| Rain | 5–15% | 10–30W |
| Snow (panels clear) | 70–90% (reflected light boost) | 140–180W |

## The 3 Panel Technologies That Matter for Low Light

### 1. Monocrystalline — The Efficiency Baseline

**Monocrystalline panels** are made from single-crystal silicon. They're more efficient than polycrystalline in all conditions, and the gap widens in low light. Monocrystalline cells convert a higher percentage of available photons into electricity — which matters most when photons are scarce.

**Why they're better for cloudy conditions:** Monocrystalline cells have a lower "temperature coefficient" and better low-light response than polycrystalline. Under overcast skies, a quality mono panel from EcoFlow, Renogy, or Jackery will consistently outperform a polycrystalline panel of the same wattage rating by 15–25%.

**The pick:** **EcoFlow 400W Rigid Solar Panel** — 23% conversion efficiency, monocrystalline cells, and built to pair with EcoFlow power stations for maximum MPPT optimization.

### 2. Bifacial — Capturing Light From Both Sides

**Bifacial panels** have a transparent backsheet that allows light to pass through and reflect off the surface behind the panel — the ground, a roof, snow cover — into the rear side of the cells. This reflected light generates additional power that a traditional opaque-backed panel simply loses.

**Why they're better for cloudy conditions:** On overcast days, light is diffuse — it comes from all directions, not just directly from the sun. Bifacial panels capture this scattered light from both sides. When the ground has reflective properties (snow, light gravel, sand, or even a light-colored roof), the gain can be 10–25% over a standard panel — and that bonus is proportionally larger on cloudy days when every watt counts.

**The pick:** **Renogy 100W Bifacial Solar Panel** — Affordable entry into bifacial technology. The transparent back and monocrystalline cells deliver up to 25% additional power from reflected light. Pairs well with any MPPT-equipped power station.

### 3. CIGS Thin-Film — The Cloudy Day Specialist

**CIGS** (Copper Indium Gallium Selenide) thin-film panels are fundamentally different from crystalline silicon panels. Instead of rigid glass-and-aluminum frames, CIGS panels are flexible, lightweight, and — most importantly for cloudy conditions — have a **superior spectral response in low and diffuse light**.

**Why they're better for cloudy conditions:** CIGS absorbs a broader spectrum of light than crystalline silicon, including the blue and green wavelengths that penetrate cloud cover more effectively. In independent testing, CIGS panels consistently outperform crystalline panels by 15–30% under heavy overcast conditions — sometimes more when mounted on non-ideal surfaces where the flexibility allows better positioning.

**The tradeoff:** CIGS panels are less efficient per square foot than crystalline panels under full sun. A 100W CIGS panel is physically larger than a 100W monocrystalline panel. If space is limited (small RV roof, compact campsite), monocrystalline may be the better choice despite lower cloudy-day performance. If you have mounting space and cloudy conditions are your main concern, CIGS wins.

**The pick:** **BougeRV CIGS Thin-Film Panels** — Available in 100W and 200W configurations. Flexible, adhesive-backed, and the best-performing panel type for consistently overcast climates.

## The Charge Controller Matters Just as Much

You can buy the best low-light panel in the world, but if it's connected to a cheap PWM charge controller, you're leaving watts on the table. **MPPT (Maximum Power Point Tracking) controllers** are essential for cloudy conditions.

Here's why: a solar panel's voltage and current vary constantly with light conditions. PWM controllers simply pass whatever the panel produces to the battery. MPPT controllers actively track the panel's optimal voltage/current combination — the "maximum power point" — and convert it to the voltage your battery needs. In full sun, MPPT is 15–25% more efficient than PWM. In cloudy conditions, that advantage can exceed 30%.

**All quality power stations in 2026 have built-in MPPT controllers.** If you're building a DIY system with separate components, budget for a quality MPPT controller (Victron, Renogy, or EPever) — it will pay for itself in recovered watts within the first overcast month.

## Real-World Strategy for Cloudy Climates

If you live somewhere with frequent cloud cover, here's the approach that works:

1. **Oversize your panel array.** If you need 200W under full sun, install 300–400W. The extra capacity offsets cloudy-day losses and gives you a buffer. Panel costs have dropped enough that oversizing is cheaper than running out of power.

2. **Use bifacial or CIGS panels.** Bifacial for ground-mount installations with reflective surfaces (light gravel, snow, white roofing). CIGS for consistently overcast climates where every watt of diffuse light matters.

3. **Pair with a quality MPPT controller.** Built into a power station or standalone. Don't cheap out on the controller — it's the difference between harvesting 15% and 25% of rated output under clouds.

4. **Angle for the season.** In winter, tilt panels steeper to capture low-angle sun and let snow slide off. In overcast conditions, a flatter tilt captures more diffuse sky light. Adjustable mounting brackets add complexity but meaningfully improve output in variable conditions.

5. **Clean snow immediately.** A dusting of snow blocks nearly all light. A clear panel surrounded by snow actually gets a bifacial-style boost from ground reflection. Clear panels after every snowfall.
